THREE KNIVES AT MIDNIGHT
A Story for Vampire: The Masquerade
As the year draws to a close, Prince Jackson of Chicago taps a traveling Carnival coterie to host the city's New Year's Eve party, and on Elysium grounds, no less. Three ambitious Kindred--Adze, Bronwyn, and Bret Stryker--circle the event, each angling for the vacant Keeper seat. As the coterie plans the event, the political knives come out, forcing them to
manage competing interests and emerge unscathed. Or, in other words, just another night in the Windy City.

CONTENT WARNING Vampire: The Masquerade addresses the darkness of the real world through horror stories. Existing fans of RPGs in general and Vampire specifically probably expect stories to contain predation on humans, blood, addiction, violence, weapons (guns), grooming, human trafficking, potential death of players' or Storyteller's characters, and betrayal. This story also includes inworld references and expressions of the following: drug use, consensual sexual situations, violence, gang references, sex work, grooming, and potential death. If you're running this story as a one-off event, consult the Advice for Considerate Play appendix in the core Vampire rulebook for advice on ensuring everyone at the table comes in with the same expectations, particularly if any players are new to Vampire.
JUMPING RIGHT IN For Storytellers running this as a standalone one-shot, the story includes a ready-to-play Carnival coterie of five vampires known for hosting Kindred events. As traveling outsiders, they have no established allies or enemies, making them ideal neutral parties for Prince Jackson's purposes. However, in Chicago, pleasing one Kindred often means disappointing another. They'll quickly discover that even party planning can become a political minefield.

Overview
Prince Jackson of Chicago wants to fill his vacant Keeper of Elysium position (or so the rumors say). Adze, Bronwyn, and Bret Stryker each had expected the title and the honor of hosting the city's New Year's Eve party when Jackson upends expectations by handing the festivities to a traveling Carnival coterie, granting them temporary access to any Chicago Elysium. Adze, Bronwyn, and Bret Stryker aren't taking the slight well and suspect more is at play. It wouldn't be the first time a Prince tested the candidates before appointing a court officer. Now the players' characters must plan the event while contending with bruised egos and a three-way cold war of wills.
Breakdown
Three Knives at Midnight follows a flexible, sandboxstyle approach in two phases. In Part One, the coterie plans the New Year's Eve celebration, making decisions about the venue, entertainment, security, and blood sources. During this phase, they also meet Adze, Bronwyn, and Bret Stryker, each offering help while pursuing their agendas. Storytellers can present these scenes in whatever order works best for their table.
Part Two shifts to the party itself. With all the pieces in play, the characters get to enjoy the evening, interacting with Storyteller characters while the consequences of their earlier choices begin to surface. Each contender's arc shifts depending on the coterie's treatment of them, influencing their behavior throughout the night. Then, when Anarchs crash the party, the story moves toward its dramatic conclusion.
A Carnival Coterie of Ancillae
This story includes a set of readytoplay ancillae belonging to a Carnival coterie. However, players are free to create their own characters, including their coterie type and whether they hail from Chicago or beyond. Carnival coteries move from place to place, bringing the party with them. Where Nomads celebrate rootlessness, Carnivals strive to make an impression on mortals, Kindred, or both, depending on the coterie's style. Some Carnivals appeal to elders by providing deliberately anachronistic entertainments, recalling Dust Bowl

circuses or, further back, medieval troubadours. Others organize raves featuring cuttingedge music spun by DJs who earn six figures a night. Not all Carnivals are devoted purely to entertainment and the chance to feed, however. Their spectacles might include politics and theater or Cainite rites, hidden in plain sight.
For more information on the Carnival coterie type, see Chicago by Night (p. 260).
CHANGING THINGS UP A LITTLE Three Knives at Midnight is written as a Camarilla story in Chicago. But perhaps the story occurs in a different city, with a different set of "knives" vying for the Keeper position. Or maybe the story takes place in an Anarch city, with the Baron tasking the coterie with hosting a party on Elysium grounds--much to the chagrin of three jealous Anarch rivals. Ultimately, the Storyteller should feel free enough to adapt the story to best serve the players' interests.

The Three Knives
With Prince Jackson asking the coterie to host a New Year's Eve party on Elysium grounds, the players' characters find themselves in a place of privilege, with many jealous eyes on them. Three sets of those eyes belong to three powerful Kindred, and how the coterie interacts with them is the core challenge of Three Knives at Midnight.
Adze, Bronwyn, and Bret Stryker are all dangerous in their own right, with sizable egos and ambitious agendas. They crave influence, and with the Keeper role in sight, they won't hesitate to backstab an ally, let alone step on the toes of a few outoftowners. The following summaries offer a snapshot of the three contenders.

ADZE, FATHER OF THE BLOOD DISCO Adze defies most Nosferatu stereotypes, enjoys threepiece suits, and has a flair for the dramatic. With an easy smile and a firm handshake, he knows how to deliver a subtle insult one moment and a VIP invitation to Red Noo 5 the next. As an established player in the game, he moves like a man who has already won. And as Father-Above-All of the Blood Disco, he commands a small army of lost and wayward souls. Everyone in Chicago is curious about who he intends to point that army at, and when.
In Three Knives at Midnight, Adze sees the coterie's assignment as a chance to test their sympathies and perhaps recruit them into his orbit. He bristles at the decorum of the Camarilla, and if the event tilts too far in favor of the city's blue bloods, he may quietly withdraw his support (or make a much louder point).

BRONWYN, SPIDER IN HER WEB Bronwyn is no recluse, and her presence in Chicago's Kindred society is deliberate and deeply entrenched. The Malkavian elder has spent centuries building a vast network of spies and informants, and her list of secrets rivals that of the Nosferatu. The Blue Velvet is hers, and few enter it without being observed, catalogued, and filed away for future use. Whether through loose lips at the club or overheard by one of her "employees" at the Circulatory System, Bronwyn's acquisition of guarded intelligence has proven her effectiveness and the inherent dangers in dealing with her.
In Three Knives at Midnight, Bronwyn does not take Prince Jackson's decision lightly. The coterie is a potential lever, and she may offer assistance while subtly elevating her status. For her, that's a win-win. But disrespect or disloyalty earns a place in her ledger, and she always collects.

The Hook
With only days left before the end of the year, the coterie is visited by a messenger on behalf of Prince Kevin Jackson of Chicago. The messenger invites the coterie to host the next New Year's Eve gathering in Chicago and offers the use of any of the city's many fine Elysia. The coterie will receive help to facilitate access, and they will be well compensated for their efforts. Choose a messenger that suits your coterie's nature: a trusted ghoul, Sheriff Damien's runner, a mortal go-between under Dominate, or even an animal messenger.
Despite the polite phrasing, Prince Jackson's message leaves little room for refusal. The coterie has a week to plan and execute Chicago's premier social event of the year. The timetable is tight, and it may leave the characters wondering why. Did the Prince's first pick fall through? Are political shenanigans afoot? Did someone call in a favor?
Storytellers can weave in any reason for the late invitation that fits their chronicle, but in the context of Three Knives at Midnight, Prince Jackson had intended to appoint a Keeper of Elysium before the new year. That individual would have hosted the gathering. Except... the information leaked, and the politics got messy. Damien and Annabelle Triabell, Bret Stryker's sire, questioned the optics of naming a Keeper without their consultation. Then, Adze pushed too hard, calling in favors on behalf of the Blood Disco. This was a move Jackson didn't appreciate. For Bronwyn's part, she leveraged her influence over the Circulatory System to quietly choke off her rivals' access to blood sources. Disgusted by the maneuvering, Jackson decided to hand New Year's Eve to a neutral party with few local ties. And now he's watching how each of the three contenders handles it.

Part One: The Plan
With a week until New Year's Eve, the coterie must choose an Elysium for the venue, organize security, source blood, and arrange entertainment. The messenger cuts through any reasonable mortal red tape, freeing players' characters to concentrate on the Kindred-specific elements. And as they plan, Adze, Bronwyn, and Bret Stryker make their moves. Each believes they should be Chicago's next Keeper of Elysium, and each sees influencing the coterie as crucial to securing that position.
Hunger: Start each character at Hunger 2 if you want immediate pressure. Otherwise, either require nightly

Rouse Checks to wake or set starting Hunger in scenes throughout the week using the freeform method from the core rules (Vampire, p. 292).
Contender Scenes
Taken in whatever order the Storyteller prefers, the three would-be Keepers reach out to the coterie. And based on the decisions the coterie makes, Adze, Bronwyn, and Stryker grow either as allies or as rivals. If the coterie makes a bad statement or uncouth display in front of any of the contenders, a test of Composure + Etiquette (Difficulty 4 for Adze, 3 for Bret or Bronwyn) may help to soothe any ruffled feathers.

TRACKING AGENDAS Each contender wants two specific things from the coterie and begins with a generally unfavorable opinion of them (whether they show it or not). As the players' characters fulfill those wants, the contender's attitude shifts. Fulfilling one want moves them to a more measured, neutral stance. Fulfilling two wants earns a supportive stance, where the contender might burn some political capital on the coterie's behalf.
These shifting opinions matter. In Part Two, the more aligned a contender is with the coterie, the more helpful they'll be during the event. Contenders with an unfavorable opinion can cause trouble and may increase the Difficulty of certain actions or trigger complications. These stances will also unlock exclusive scenes during the party.
In order to have at least one favorable or unfavorable opinion, the Storyteller may interpret these loosely or strictly. Should everyone's opinion be neutral, consider if there are other reasons one of these three would be favorably or unfavorably inclined towards the coterie. Adze wants:  The local Anarchs invited to the party  The Blood Disco featured as the headline entertainment  Sanctioned midnight feeding per Blood Disco rites Bronwyn wants:  The Blue Velvet selected as the venue  The Circulatory System to supply the blood  Strict rules for Anarchs--mandatory escort, no feeding Bret Stryker wants:  The Neo-Futurist Theater, the City Ballet, the Art Museum, the Opera Theater, or the Symphony Orchestra chosen for the venue  Baby Chorus featured as the headline entertainment  No visible feeding, no photography, and no video allowed The coterie could go all in with a contender and come out with a true ally, or they could hedge their bets and satisfy a little something from each.

Adze at Red Noº 5
Adze invites the coterie to party with him at Red Noº 5.
"Names?" The doorman to Red Noº 5 flicks his attention between you and his tablet. His earpiece crackles. He nods, then steps aside. "They're cleared."
Inside, you step into a haze of sweat, soul, and pulsing bass. A woman in a sharp suit leads you to a side lounge area where Adze waits. His round glasses catch the club's shifting lights as he gestures for you to sit.
ROLEPLAYING ADZE You don't deal in subtleties. You play it straight, you speak your truth (albeit with a flair for the dramatic), and you call people out on their bullshit. You may play the game, but you don't give a flying fuck what people think of you. And you're not afraid to burn a bridge or cuss someone out for disrespecting you and yours. You'll offer a fair deal, and you don't go out of your way to make enemies, but you don't give an inch if someone gets on your bad side.

ADZE'S AGENDA
While irritated with Prince Jackson, Adze is open to working with the coterie. He wants two things. He wants the musicians and artists from the Blood Disco to be the featured entertainment for the night. He views this as an easy and straightforward way to raise his profile and build influence with his allies. Additionally, he wants some local Anarchs invited to the event. A few months back, an Anarch named Victoria Longwood tagged the Blue Velvet with spray paint to help others find Elysium. The Camarilla staked her for it, and now the Anarchs are especially furious at Prince Jackson, Bronwyn, and Sheriff Damien. Adze doesn't push hard, but he strongly suggests the coterie invite a Brujah Anarch named Gengis and his crew to cool tensions and needle Bronwyn.
A successful Intelligence + Politics or Streetwise test at Difficulty 2 reminds the coterie that Adze is a notable up-and-comer in the Chicago scene. Various oppressed Duskborn, angry Anarchs, and even a few disgruntled Camarilla Kindred regularly frequent the Red Noº 5. Bronwyn at the Blue Velvet
The Malkavian elder invites the coterie to the Blue Velvet but is diplomatic enough to meet with the coterie at their convenience.
Tucked into a corner of the Rack, the entrance to the Blue Velvet is a discreet door on Rush Street. Deep-blue silk drapes hang in perfect folds above the polished mahogany of four separate bars, tables, and chairs. Every glass is cut crystal. Bronwyn stands as you enter, smooth and calm, before gesturing toward a curved settee across from her.
ROLEPLAYING BRONWYN A true elder in every sense of the word, you don't rise to the bait. You have worked very hard to carve out a niche for yourself, and that niche has provided you with an endless cache of secrets. You don't forget. Not names. Not favors. Not slights. Especially not slights. This coterie might be useful, or they might be noise. You'll know soon enough. Find out which and move on to the next item on your agenda. Make them earn your trust.
BRONWYN'S AGENDA
Bronwyn wants the coterie to host the event at the Blue Velvet. She's widely assumed to be the top candidate for the Keeper position. Hosting the event at the Blue Velvet signals to the city's Kindred that she's already doing the job regardless of titles. Offering her club also allows her to bundle in free access to the Circulatory System, which makes sourcing blood easy and provides

her with a direct link to track and observe guests. A successful Wits + Insight test at Difficulty 5 helps the coterie put two and two together. The Malkavian is a collector of secrets, after all.
Using the Blue Velvet:
"I know you're considering other offers," Bronwyn says. "But let me be clear, I' d be honored if you hosted your event here. And, unlike others, I can offer you something tangible in return--my connections to the Circulatory System. No need to roll in crates of pre-processed blood and pray the gathered Kindred can stomach such a weak offering. No mind games. No pesky blood dolls. My people are clean, compliant, and above all, discreet."
The Circulatory System:
"The Circulatory System provides a vital service. A safe way for Kindred to feed without compromising the Masquerade. The alternative is unrestricted hunting throughout Chicago. Tell me how that better serves anyone's interests." She frowns just a touch. "But if the Blue Velvet isn't your venue choice, I understand. The System is still available for a fair price. Let's say, a substantial favor. Or something equivalent."
An Intelligence + Insight or Politics test (Difficulty 2) confirms that Bronwyn is offering the coterie the use of the Circulatory System for one night for a Major Boon, but only if the coterie opts out of using the Blue Velvet. If they go with the Blue Velvet, they can access the Circulatory System for free.
Victoria Longwood: Meanwhile, a Wits + Streetwise test (Difficulty 3) reminds the coterie about the fate of Victoria Longwood, which they may have heard about on their own or from Adze.

However, the decision to stake her was Prince Jackson's, not mine. The consequences of that action rest entirely with the Prince and his Sheriff. I merely maintain my establishment according to Camarilla custom."

BRET STRYKER'S AGENDA

Decisions
With the contender scenes concluded, the coterie has some decisions to make, along with potentially deploying Influence and rolling some tests. Remember to keep track of how the decisions impact the three Keeper contenders. Refer back to Tracking Agendas (p. 9).
Elysium
If a player's character has any amount of Status or a Mawla in the city, they acquire a list of the known Elysia in Chicago. That list includes Garfield Park Conservatory, the Neo-Futurist Theater, the Chicago City Ballet, the Chicago Central Library, the Chicago Opera Theater, the Chicago Symphony Orchestra, the Linda Enfield Art Gallery, the Art Museum of Chicago, the Smart Gallery, the Arie Crown Theater, the Civic Theater, and the Blue Velvet. Midway Airport is also nominally Elysium but under lupine control.
However, the Storyteller shouldn't feel bound to this list of choices and should feel free to add or remove locations from this list. Per Prince Jackson, the coterie has free rein and complete access over their intended Elysium. Ideally, the players should lean into their creative side, sprucing up the Elysium in whatever aesthetic interests them.
Scouting various Elysium locations with an Intelligence + Streetwise or Politics test (Difficulty 2) may reveal a location that can partly satisfy (or direct a middle finger to) one of the three contenders at the Storyteller's discretion. On a success, reveal one superficial advantage that would please a contender. For every +2 margin, reveal one additional advantage (Storyteller's choice). Examples: easy ingress and egress for entertainment or blood, proximity suitable for an elite afterparty, or a venue the contender was recently spotted genuinely enjoying.

THE LAWS OF ELYSIUM While the rules governing Elysium are largely consistent throughout the Camarilla, Chicago has four courtesies that have persisted for decades. They are listed below, but further details can be found in Chicago by Night (p. 64). 1. On pain of a Blood Hunt, no violence is permitted on
the premises against Kindred, kine, or property. 2. Elysium is to be considered neutral territory. No
conflict of any sort among Kindred may be carried onto its sacred ground. 3. Access shall not be limited; all Cainites are welcome. 4. One should not attract attention as one leaves and enters Elysium.
WHAT ABOUT THE SUCCUBUS CLUB? While the Succubus Club is technically an Elysium, that status is debatable. In 1993, the lupines swept through the Succubus Club, killing several Kindred and causing so much damage that the building was shuttered for twenty-five years. It has since been remodeled and reopened, but the question remains: Is it still an Elysium?
Per Bret Stryker, the answer is yes and no (Chicago by Night, p. 28). That ambiguity offers some great flexibility for storytelling, but it may be less helpful when presenting clear choices to players. There's a case for including the Succubus Club. It's iconic, and some players may love throwing a party in such a notorious venue. On the other hand, they might want to use a fresh location that hasn't seen as much action.
Ultimately, Storytellers should decide if the Succubus Club is or is not a formal Elysium, and if so, offer it as a potential location for the players' characters.
Entertainment
Stryker wants Baby Chorus (Chicago by Night, p. 237) to headline the event. Adze wants the spotlight on the Blood Disco (Chicago by Night, p. 241). Each group represents a very different style of entertainment, and choosing one over the other undoubtedly infuriates the rival. An alternative is for the coterie to hire an entirely different band or to perform themselves. The Storyteller should use discretion when determining how Adze and Stryker respond. By default, selecting neither group should keep both contenders at an unfavorable or measured stance. Blood
Feeding guests poses a logistical challenge. Bronwyn, through her ties to the Circulatory System, provides a convenient solution, albeit with a moral quandary. If the characters choose to host the event at the Blue Velvet, she can make her blood dolls available for free. If the party is elsewhere, she may still offer several dozen mortal hors d'oeuvres, but at a price. The Blood Disco also means free feeding at midnight, while the Baby Chorus must be persuaded to waive their demand for no feeding within a three-block radius of the gig.
If the coterie declines, they'll need to source blood independently. That might involve tapping into personal backgrounds (i.e., Herd) or local connections. And they'll need to research guest preferences to get an idea of Kindred tastes. Options include, but are not limited to:  Animalism to trap animals for Farmers.
 Medical Allies or Influence to secure processed blood for Baggers.
 Presence to seduce mortals into offering their blood. These mortals will need to be drugged, blindfolded, or disposed of to avoid endangering the Masquerade.
Relying solely on bagged or animal blood will leave most guests very unhappy. On the other hand, mortals aren't inclined to give up their blood to strangers. They'll need to be coerced, incentivized, or compelled. And most importantly, the coterie must clean up the mess afterward lest they raise Masquerade concerns. In short, someone needs to take care of these people. Storytellers are encouraged to lean hard into the ramifications of the players' characters' choices regarding their Humanity and the Second Inquisition.
Security
While the coterie doesn't need to guard the door themselves, they are responsible for ensuring the event is secure. This includes deciding who makes it onto the guest list and who doesn't. In Chicago, Elysium is open to all, but the VIP floor is not. Is everyone welcome? Are Anarchs invited? What about Duskborn? Once they finalize the list, Prince Jackson's messenger handles the invitations.
Chicago has a large Kindred population, and even with Chicago by Night in hand, tracking every guest can be overwhelming. Storytellers shouldn't feel pressured to portray every Primogen or named Storyteller character.

Assume the people the coterie wants to be there made it to the event. If characters are important to the players' ongoing chronicle, they can be there, too. Beyond that, this story requires Adze, Bronwyn, and Bret Stryker to make an appearance. Sheriff Damien could also be there if Baby Chorus performs. And finally, Prince Jackson shows up toward the end.
Given that the party is occurring on New Year's Eve, the streets of Chicago will be busy. Some options for security include:  Animalism to deploy scouts around the venue.
 Resources to hire private security and crowd control.
 Allies or Influence in law enforcement to keep cops clear of the area.
 Streetwise or Influence on the streets to coordinate with local gangs.
Any meaningful preparation as determined by the Storyteller will act as an early warning system for any unwanted guests in Part Two. Additionally, if the characters hire Baby Chorus to headline the entertainment, they may also benefit from the fact that the band's lead singer, Damien, is also the Sheriff.
